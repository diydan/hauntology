Chapter Two

Four reasons to come in

Four reasons, counted twice to be sure.

Reason one

Your place, your game

Turn a photo of any place you know into the haunted house you play in. You will know every creaking stair.

Reason two

Be the detective

Collect clues, build your accusation and name who is haunting the place. Do it politely. The ghost is listening.

Reason three

Always in the game

Off your turn, stir up creaks and whispers, then help or hinder whoever is on the clock. Nobody has to sit and wait.

Reason four

Join the ghost

Guess wrong and you join the haunting. You can't win any more, but you haunt your friends instead of sitting out. It is a strange sort of promotion.

Chapter Three

Come alone, or bring company

Two ways to play. Neither is quiet.

You and the ghost

Single player

Walk into your photo, design the ghost that haunts it, and talk to it until it gives up its three memories. It is shy at first, so be patient.

  • Draw or upload the place
  • Design the ghost yourself
  • Take your time, there is no clock

Two to six players

Multiplayer

Friends and family around one screen, each on their own phone. Bots fill any empty seat, so nobody faces the house alone.

  • Scan a code to join
  • Everyone has a part to play on every turn
  • Spooky, never gory

        Chapter Six

        Even the phones join the haunting

        Phones creak, glow and whisper. All of it is optional, every player says yes for themselves, and none of it ever changes a rule.

        Chapter Seven

        Get it wrong, and you haunt your friends

        The Haunt meter climbs every round and every time a turn clock runs out. At 3, 6 and 9 the house does something, and if the meter fills, the ghost wins.

        Accusations are sealed and revealed together at the end of the round, so going first is no advantage. Accuse wrongly and you join the haunting: you keep your seat, shake the house and choose what it does next, but you can no longer win. The best haunter gets a title at the end.

        • nounThe Haunt The number on the meter. It is not the same as dread, though the two often arrive together.
        • verbTo join the haunting What happens after a wrong accusation. You keep your seat, lose the right to win, and gain the right to creak.
        At 9 the meter is one step from full, and the house acts. I advise standing well back.

        Chapter Eight

        Room for friends and family

        A game you can start in the time it takes to scan a code. Best started before dark.

        • Start from any photoA holiday spot, your old school, your living room. A sketch works too.
        • Two to six playersBots fill any empty seat, so a small group still has a full table.
        • One screen, many phonesA TV or laptop shows the world. Phones do everything else, with no app store.
        • Single player?It puts you in the place with a ghost to talk to. On a laptop you can also play the party game with Bots.
        • Spooky, never goryThe haunting is gentle. The question is who is haunting the place, and nobody is harmed.
        • Bring a 3D printerPrinted relics and a hologram pyramid are optional extras.

        Appendix

        The small print, in plain words

        None of this is the game. All of it is why the game is safe to hand to a family. Eight parts, all counted.

        These are promises the build is held to, each one written down with a test before any code.

        Your photos and placesPrivacy

        • Cleaned firstLocation and camera data are stripped from a photo before anything else happens. If a photo has a date and time, only that is read, to suggest a day to remember.
        • Checked, and refused if unsurePhotos and typed words are checked before use. If the check cannot run, the answer is no.
        • Short-livedPhotos are kept 24 hours and worlds 7 days. “Delete my place” removes everything at once. A party room and everything in it is deleted when the game ends, or after 6 hours.
        • Blurry places on purposeOnly a rounded position is kept (two decimals, about a kilometre). It is never shown to other players and never written to a log.
        • No accounts, no analyticsSeat tokens are unguessable and work for one seat only. Logs hold ids and counters, never your words, your audio or your photo.
        • Your words are only wordsAnything typed is treated as plain data in a length-capped block, never as instructions to the ghost.

        The ghostSafety

        • The engine decides, the model narratesThe rules run in plain code. If the model is slow, fails or says something unsuitable, a scripted line takes its place and the game does not change.
        • Every line is checkedBefore a line is shown or spoken it is checked for safety. It never mentions news, crime, death or a real person or address.
        • Always finishableGallery worlds, preset ghosts and scripted lines let anyone play a whole game with no quota and no model calls.

        Money and limitsCost

        • A ticket for every paid callEach paid call needs a ticket reserved first, with caps per session, per day and in total, and a kill switch. When a cap is hit, the free parts keep working.
        • Cached, not repeatedA spoken line is cached for 30 days. The weather is read once per game. A repeated scripted line costs one call in total.
        • Budgets for the noisy bitsA party room speaks at most 20 lines. Each phone gets at most 3 sounds a round, and the table at most 6 cues a turn.

        The real worldFrozen inputs

        • One snapshotWeather, hour and moon are read once at the start and frozen, so every player sees the same sky. They change looks and the ghost’s words, never a rule. If the weather does not arrive within 3 seconds, the sky is clear.
        • Stars with no serviceThe stars are worked out on the device from a built-in list of up to 300, drawn once into a small picture, so the sky costs nothing per frame.
        • Same seed, same resultChance is seeded, so a light show, a sky or a draw can be replayed exactly, which is how it gets tested.

        Phones, sound and lightLittle tech

        • Light timingEach phone measures how far its clock is from the server. A light cue starts half a second ahead, and a phone that is badly out of step is skipped rather than played late. The numbers are tuned on real phones.
        • Gentle by designAt most two brightness changes a second, fades only, greys and whites, a peak below full white, and none at all for anyone who asks for reduced motion.
        • A shake has to be a shakeIt counts after three swings in a second and a half. A bump on the table or a phone in a pocket does not.
        • Limits belong to the seatYour limits stay with you if your phone drops and reconnects. Nobody gets a fresh start by refreshing.
        • Lens that stays cheapThe spirit lens draws simple sprites at 30 frames a second at most, and pauses when the phone is face down or the page is hidden.
        • Bounded inputSticks and look controls are clamped, and a phone sends at most 30 messages a second.

        Secrets and fairnessTrust

        • Hidden cards never leave the serverYour phone only ever receives your own cards. A test plants a secret marker in every hidden card and checks that it never reaches anyone else.
        • The server owns the clockPhones show the turn clock but do not decide it. No one can shorten a turn to less than 30 seconds in total, whatever they do.
        • Your voice stays yoursAudio is deleted as soon as it is turned into text. The only clips ever kept are the short words for the whisper game, only from players who said yes, and they are deleted when the game ends. A parent or guardian is asked to say yes for a child.

        Everyone can playAccess

        • Sound is never the only clueEvery sound has a written line beside it, and the ghost always has subtitles. The narration is announced politely for screen readers.
        • Reduced motion is honouredFlicker, light shows and motion all switch off or soften for anyone who asks.
        • Plain words“Single player”, “Multiplayer” and “joined the haunting”. No jargon at the table.
        • No app storePhones join by scanning a code and run in the browser. A weaker device gets a lighter world automatically.

        How it is builtStack and method

        • Mostly CloudflareWorkers, Durable Objects (one per game room), R2 storage, Workers AI for words, pictures, safety checks and voice, and Cloudflare Images for the ghost cut-out. World Labs makes the 3D world and Open-Meteo supplies the weather.
        • Specs firstSeven specs hold 287 promises and 124 correctness properties, each with its own test. A script fails the build if any promise has no task.
        • Pure rulesThe rules are plain functions with no network, and the clock and the dice are passed in, so every rule can be tested on its own.
        • Honest limitsWorlds are static, the ghost is a flat sprite, and the hologram is an optical illusion tuned on real hardware.
