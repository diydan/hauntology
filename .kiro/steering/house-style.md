# House style: look, sound and voice (decided 2026-11-16)

Hauntology is a gothic storybook: morbid but sweet, deadpan and exact, sad ghosts with small dark jokes. It draws on the feel of three makers of that kind of work (a gothic film director, a symmetric deadpan film director and a book series with a sorry narrator), and copies none of them.

**Never:** a named character, a title, a quote, a song, a melody, an image or a logo from anyone's work; and never a real person's name in a prompt to a model ("in the style of …"). Describe the traits below instead. Everything we make is our own.

## Look
- Shapes tall, thin and a little crooked; curls and spirals; houses that lean and have too many windows.
- Ink lines that wobble a little, as if drawn by hand; black and white stripes and checker floors as patterns.
- Mostly black, grey and the browns of `design/tokens.json`, with one bright pop: our yellow. No new colour without the design-system spec.
- Neat and centred where it counts: title cards, menus and the ending are symmetric and framed, like a stage.
- Ghosts are sweet, sad and a little funny; never gory, never a jump scare.
- Motion: small sways and wobbles, like paper puppets, within the motion limits of `visual-style.md`; none under reduced motion.
- Type: a spindly, hand-drawn feel for titles only; body text stays plain and readable.

## Sound (songs, stings and loops)
- Instruments: celesta, music box, toy piano, tuba, plucked strings, a little organ, a bell.
- Voices: a child choir's wordless "ooh", a cracked baritone, a sweet sad soprano.
- Rhythm: a waltz in three, a lurching march, a slow lullaby.
- Mood: spooky but cosy; a lullaby with a wink. Never loud scares.
- Words: story rhymes, a little morbid, a little funny, in the narrator's voice.

## Voice (narration, log lines, recap, lyrics)
1. The narrator speaks to the players, politely, like a guide who is sorry about what comes next.
2. Rounds are **chapters**, each with a title card: "Chapter Three, in which Agnes goes to the Cellar."
3. Exact, odd numbers: "eleven stairs", "three minutes past the hour".
4. Now and then the narrator explains a word, dryly: "A *séance*, which here means a great deal of sitting in the dark."
5. Deadpan: the stranger the event, the plainer the sentence.
6. Kind to every player; unkind only to the house.
7. Short lines that read aloud well; captions always.
8. Never a hint the rules do not allow; the narrator knows only what the public log knows, until the ending.

UI chrome (buttons, labels, settings) stays short and plain, as `visual-style.md` says: the narrator speaks in narration, not on buttons.
