# Hauntology — tech stack

## Languages and tooling
- TypeScript (strict) everywhere. pnpm workspaces. Vitest + fast-check for tests. zod for runtime schemas.
- `web/`: Vite + React + three.js (r180+) + `@sparkjsdev/spark` + `three-mesh-bvh`.
- `api/`: Cloudflare Workers + Hono. Durable Objects: `SpendGuard`, `WorldJob`, `PlaySession`, `PartyRoom` (WebSocket Hibernation API), `PartyDirectory` (singleton for room codes and caps). R2 for blobs. KV only for non-critical caches.
- `shared/`: pure TypeScript (no I/O, no Date.now/Math.random inside logic; inject clock and rng). This is where property tests live.
- Pin exact versions in the lockfile at install time; do not guess version numbers.

All AI runs on Cloudflare: text, vision and moderation through the `env.AI` Workers AI binding, the sprite cut-out through Cloudflare Images (the `env.IMAGES` binding, which needs an Images Paid subscription). Neither binding is declared in `api/wrangler.jsonc` until the owner decides to spend: in `wrangler dev` the `AI` binding always reaches the real, billed service. There are no AI keys, no regions and no request signing, and AWS (Bedrock, Nova) is not used anywhere. One `WorkersAiClient` in `api/src/guarded/workersai/` (built, world-pipeline 5.2) wraps `env.AI.run(modelId, input)` and the Images call; each of its adapters is off when its binding or model id is missing. Every function takes a `SpendTicket`, and every call has a scripted fallback.

## Unverified assumptions (each has a spike; do not build on them blindly)
1. Whether upstream asset URLs expire, and the formats of the panorama and thumbnail. The field names for splat, collider mesh, pano, thumbnail and the scale metadata are now documented (`worldlabs-api.md`, `docs/spikes/1.1.md`) but not yet confirmed by a run; Spike 1.1 has not been run (it needs a World API key and credits).
2. Whether FLUX.2 klein 9B (`IMAGE_MODEL_ID`) produces acceptable sketch-to-photo quality from a 512 px sketch used as a reference image (the model page now says reference images must be smaller than 512 by 512, and the shell sends 512 on the longest side; the call is written from the page's multipart example in `api/src/guarded/workersai/image.ts` and tested over fakes, not called), and whether that is good enough for World Labs to build from. If not, switch to `flux-2-dev`. Spike 1.2 retires this.
3. Splat/world orientation: documented, not yet seen in a viewer. The files are in the `marble_raw_opencv` frame, the service turns them half a turn about x, and gives a scale and a ground height per world (`worldlabs-api.md`, `poseFromService`); one real world in the viewer must confirm upright, facing and floor (needed for up-vector and eye height).
4. Whether outpaint-synthesized extra views improve world quality over a single photo.
5. Spark API surface for opacity/recolor/LOD in the installed version.
6. Party Mode: WebSocket p50/p95 input latency through a Durable Object with six phones at 30 Hz (party Spike 1.1).
7. Party Mode: iOS and Android multitouch, orientation permission, vibration, camera QR scan reliability on single-colour relief plates (party Spike 1.2).
8. Party Mode: pyramid orientation, mirroring and calibration on real hardware (party Spike 1.3).
9. The ghost voice: how Inworld TTS 2 is billed on this account and its rate limits, its latency on a deployed Worker (at most 5 s from request to audio, else the subtitle), which Aura-2 speakers best fit as the fallback, and Whisper clip limits and cost (ghost-voice Spike 1.1). The voices and both Steering Tags were confirmed by listening to samples on 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15.
10. Whether Scout (`GHOST_MODEL_ID`) returns the place brief and a safe or unsafe verdict from a photo as clean JSON, what its image size limits are, and what shape its plain text answer has (`{response}` or the OpenAI-style `choices`; `generateText` reads both) (Spike 1.2). The check is written (`api/src/guarded/workersai/scout.ts`, `shared/src/world/brief.ts`) from the documented chat and JSON-mode shapes and tested over fakes; the way a photo goes into a message and what JSON mode answers with a photo are not confirmed by a call. It needs the `AI` binding, which `wrangler dev` always reaches for real, so it is not declared in `wrangler.jsonc` until the owner decides to spend.
11. Whether Llama Guard 3 is accurate enough on short text such as nicknames, and what to do with languages it does not list. The spec already fails closed when moderation fails. The check is written (`api/src/guarded/workersai/guard.ts`, `shared/src/world/ai-answers.ts`) from the documented chat call and the documented answer (the text `safe` or `unsafe` and codes, or `{safe, categories}`) and tested over fakes; the real answer shape and the codes it gives for harmless short text are not confirmed by a call, and every code blocks until they are (Spike 1.2).
12. Whether Cloudflare Images `segment=foreground` works from a Worker with clean transparency and at what cost. The page documents it for the URL form and `cf.image` (BiRefNet through Workers AI, billed as an ordinary unique transformation, no neurons); the cut-out is written against the Worker `IMAGES` binding (`api/src/guarded/workersai/cutout.ts`), whose page does not list `segment`, and the default `wrangler dev` runs an offline binding with only width, height, rotate and format, so it needs `wrangler dev --remote` or a deployed Worker, an Images Paid subscription, and the owner declaring the binding. If the binding refuses it, the fallback is a `fetch` with `cf.image` from a URL the service can reach; the real cost per AI call (Workers AI bills in neurons, not dollars) to size the image meter caps; and that the klein 9B zero-data-retention listing holds, confirmed before any sketch is sent (Spike 1.2).
13. Party Mode: whether one tap at join unlocks Web Audio on iOS and Android for the rest of the game, whether the six synthesised Cues are good enough through a phone speaker, and how well Restless at 400 ms steps holds across six phones (party Spike 1.4).
14. Party Mode balance: the real share of Turns Spooked, the timeout rate, the Haunt's speed and Eavesdrop use under Classic and Gentle (party Spike 1.5).
15. Open-Meteo from a Worker: geocoding, sunrise and sunset, archive data, rate limits (conditions Spike 1.1). The star list uses simple low-precision formulas, fine for a look and not for astronomy.
16. How often Whisper rejects a clean one-second word from a phone microphone, and how the joined Stitch clips sound (ghost-voice Spike 1.2).
17. A live voice transport on Cloudflare (WebRTC, a WebSocket or Realtime), end-of-speech detection and the real delay; the 2 to 3 seconds from the end of a sentence to the first spoken word is an estimate (ghost-voice Spike 1.3, stretch).
18. Phones as lens, light and shake: gyro drift, iOS motion permission, battery cost, the clock offset between phones (500 ms lead and 150 ms drop are starting guesses), wake lock support, and how often a bump on a table looks like a shake (party Spikes 1.7 and 1.8).
19. Selfie ghosts (optional): whether `google/nano-banana-2.1` keeps an input image (the pages for Nano Banana 2 and Pro say zero data retention, the page for 2.1 does not), the real cost and latency per selfie, how well it keeps a likeness in the hand-coloured style (the selfie ghost is in colour, and turns black and white through a greyscale filter when its Player joins the haunting), and whether a small WASM face detector runs well on a mid-range phone (party spike to add; the EU treatment of a face is for the owner and a lawyer, not this list).

When an assumption is resolved, record it in `docs/spikes/<n>.md` and update this list.
