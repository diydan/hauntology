# character-builder

A viewer for the whole ModularAnimeCharacter pack: male and female bodies, four heads each, 19 hairstyles plus bangs and tied-hair pieces (and hat-fitted versions), three hats, two glasses, three scarves, twelve tops, six bottoms, four outfits and seven shoes. Every option is a picture in a sideways-scrolling strip, and everything can be recoloured with one colour wheel. Legs, arms and build have sliders. The character sings, raps, or plays guitar, electric guitar, keyboard or drums (with the instrument), blinks naturally and lip-syncs.

**The pack's files and the Mixamo clips are not in this folder and must not be committed.** `assets/` is git-ignored. Licence: the pack is cleared for commercial use by whoever supplied it; Mixamo clips cannot be redistributed.

## Build the assets
1. `cd ../prepare-assets && node prepare_modular.mjs <ModularAnimeCharacter folder> ../character-builder/assets`
   Reads the pack's `.meta`/`.mat` files and writes `mesh/` (the FBX parts), `tex/` (masks as PNG, normals as WebP, both at most 1024 px: about 18 MB instead of 80 MB) and `manifest.json` (catalogue, material colours, which FBX uses which material).
2. Clips, from your own Mixamo downloads, one file per body (`Singing`, `Rapping`, `Guitar Playing`, `Guitar Playing (1)`, `Piano Playing (seated)` (shown as Keyboard), `Playing Drums (seated)`):
   `node retarget_cc.mjs ../character-builder/assets/mesh/M_body.fbx <dir with the Mixamo .fbx files> ../character-builder/assets/clips_M.glb` (and the same with `F_body.fbx` for `clips_F.glb`).
3. Serve this folder (`npx serve .`) and open it. For a host that cannot serve `.fbx`/`.glb`, add a `<file>.b64.txt` copy of each; the page falls back to it.
4. The tile pictures: `npm i playwright`, then `node make-thumbs.cjs` (about ten minutes; `ONLY=hair,top` and `GENDERS=F` narrow it, `THREE_ROOT=<node_modules/three>` serves three.js from disk). It opens the page in its `?thumbs` mode (flat background, A-pose), sets each option on a plain base look and writes `assets/thumb/<M|F>/<kind>_<id>.webp`. Without the pictures the tiles show their names.

## How it works
- **Loading.** While it loads the loader names what is being fitted (Choosing pants, Ironing shirt, Shining shoes, Brushing hair ...). The first load, and a change of body, keep the character hidden behind a smoke effect with a progress bar until the body and every chosen piece are in, then clear the smoke to reveal it. After that, a changed part (hat, hair, top ...) is loaded completely before the old one is removed, so nothing ever flashes bare. A whole new look (Randomise on the same body) is swapped in as one step. Pieces loaded for a request that was overtaken by a newer click are thrown away.
- **Parts load one at a time.** Only the chosen body, head, hair and clothes are fetched (a typical look is about 5 MB), not the 29 MB of meshes.
- **Strips.** Each option is a tile in a strip; drag with the mouse, swipe, use the arrows, or scroll. Choosing a top or bottom takes an outfit off. Mood tiles use the pack's expression blendshapes.
- **Colour.** The pack's shader is reproduced in `onBeforeCompile`. A mask texture's red, green and blue channels pick three regions, each blended between two colours. Every colour in the panel (skin, eyes, hair, and the Main / Trim / Detail regions of each item) uses the same chip, which opens one hue-ring and saturation/value-square wheel with a hex field and a Default button. Regions are ranked by how much of the item they cover, so *Main* is the largest.
- **Bangs and ties.** The pack's bangs, buns, ponytail and twintails are loose pieces (a bald head with just that piece on it), so they are an add-on slot laid over the chosen hairstyle. The tied pieces switch the hairstyle to the tied style they were made for.
- **Face.** Eye size, nose length and ear tuck are the head's own blendshapes. The four heads of each body are nearly the same mesh (mean vertex difference about 0.2 mm, at most 6 mm around the eyes and brows, same materials), so the head tiles look alike.
- **Body shape.** Legs and Arms (short to long) and Build (Slim, Lean, Standard, Athletic, Large) re-skin the body instead of scaling bones at run time: in the rest pose each limb and torso bone gets a scale along or across its own axis, every bone origin moves with its parent's scaled offset, and the vertices of the body and of every garment follow by linear blend skinning from the old rest pose to the new one. The skeleton is then rebound to the new rest pose (rotations unchanged, scales 1), so the clips play on it without shear. Hands, feet and head keep their size. A longer or shorter leg moves the character up or down so the feet stay on the floor, and the props stay where they are.
- **Fit.** Clothes are skinned to the body's own bones and rebound by name. Body pieces are pulled in 0.6 to 1.3 cm along their normals so clothes sit over skin without poking through; vertices shared between pieces move together so no cracks open at the seams. The torso, upper torso and shoulders are not moved because they overlap each other.
- **Hats.** Choosing a hat swaps the hair for its hat-fitted version; the racing helmet hides the hair and the add-ons.
- **Blinking:** the head's `eyes_closed.01` morph, every 2 to 6 seconds (quick close, short hold, slower open), with an occasional double blink.
- **Lip sync (fake):** while the clip is Singing or Rapping, random syllables drive `jaw_open` and one of the `phoneme_*` morphs, with the lips closing (`phoneme_MBP`) now and then; rapping is faster.
- **Props.** The microphone is only there for Singing and Rapping; it is pinned just in front of the mouth while the fist is near and rides the hand otherwise. The other clips get their own instrument, built from simple shapes and fitted to the clip by sampling it: the guitars hang from the chest bone with the neck along the line from the strumming hand to the fretting hand and the body facing the way the chest faces; the keyboard sits under the hands on a desk with a solid front panel down to the floor, with a stool under the hips; the drum kit (snare where the hands come down, two toms, floor tom, kick between the knees, hi-hat and two cymbals, stool, sticks in the hands) is placed around the lowest points of the hands. The Prop button hides the instrument.
